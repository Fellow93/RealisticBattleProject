# Changelog

## v4.7.1 (changes since v4.7.0)

### Performance
- Better battle performance, especially in large battles: fewer stutters when armies deploy and reinforcements arrive, at first contact and at the first volleys, and less work every frame in a big melee.

### Combat
- **No more "magnet" shields in custom battles:** in custom battles a raised shield caught arrows and other missiles in a large invisible zone around it (the base game's catch size), so missiles that would have missed were pulled into it. It now catches only what actually hits it, as in the campaign.

### AI
- **Reinforcements link up with the men already fighting:**
  - The rally after a reinforcement wave no longer pulls back men who are still in melee, and no longer turns the rest of them away from the fight to run back toward the wave. A formation whose survivors are still fighting doesn't rally at all.
  - A rally now ends once the men who aren't fighting have closed up, instead of always running its full 45 seconds.
  - The rally now always gathers on the bulk of the formation (usually the fresh wave). A large group of survivors could be mistaken for the main body, so the wave was sent forward to them, or the formation switched back and forth between the two.
  - The rally's short window right after a wave, in which a smaller gap is enough to start a rally, now opens for every wave. A wave that landed while the formation was charging used to miss it.
- **Shield walls move faster:**
  - A charging shield wall walks only within about 30 m of the enemy. Its men further back, such as a fresh reinforcement wave, move at full charge speed instead of walking the whole way at half speed.
  - A spread-out shield wall or square advancing in formation now moves at half the running speed of its slower men instead of 0.3 of it, which was a crawl of about 1 m/s.

### Campaign
- **Towns and castles raise and pay for their own patrols.** In the base game only a town with a Guard House had a patrol, it cost nothing, and its men were replaced for free whenever it went home; under RBM its men even drew a full day's wage into their spoils out of thin air. Now:
  - Every town and castle can keep a patrol, Guard House or not, if its own money can pay for it: its treasury, and in a town also the citizens' savings above the reserve they keep. Your clan's gold is never touched.
  - Its size follows that money: a share of it each day (the new Patrol Budget Share setting, default 0.02) divided by what one soldier costs a day, between 12 and 20 men. The money is averaged over the last week, so a few days' swing in a town's citizens' savings neither disbands nor shrinks its patrol. The Guard House (+50% per level, in towns), the Coastal Guard Edict (+25%, port towns) and the Patrols perk of the settlement's governor, or of its owner when it has none (+25%), raise both the money spent and the most men allowed, up to 60. The patrol's party size tooltip shows how its size was worked out.
  - Its quality follows the same bonuses: RBM's four patrol levels, the first without a Guard House and one more for each Guard House level (the edict and the perk can tip it up a level).
  - Its men come from the settlement's garrison first: the patrol's own kind of soldier, or else a garrison man of the same tier and the same kind (mounted, ranged or foot), or else one of the same kind a tier lower, who brings his experience and his share of the spoils with him. The garrison always keeps at least 50 healthy men. Only the men the garrison cannot spare are recruited, at a garrison recruit's price and from the settlement's recruit pool, and paid out of the same money the patrol's size is worked from (the treasury, then the citizens' savings), which must hold five times a man's price for each one and still pay for a patrol that size once he is paid for.
  - A patrol topping up at home keeps the men it has (and their spoils) instead of being rebuilt from scratch, and fills its gaps the same way, garrison first. Men who were promoted on patrol, or who came from an earlier, weaker patrol, still count for their places, so they are not recruited a second time. If neither the garrison nor the recruits can bring it back near full strength, it goes back out short instead of waiting at home; men it then loses in the field draw it home again, and it tries again whenever it comes home (or after a week at most). A patrol that cannot even be brought back up to 12 men is stood down, and the settlement raises a new one when it can. The settlement pays its men's full wage and upkeep every day, coastal patrols included.
  - A patrol stood down because its settlement can no longer pay for it, or because the settlement changed hands peacefully (a gift, a trade, a ruler's grant, a clan leaving its kingdom), sends its men, wounded included, back into the garrison with their experience and spoils. A settlement taken by siege or rebellion loses its patrol as before. A patrol home with more men than its settlement now pays for sends the extra men back to the garrison too.
  - Patrols march faster: +0.5 map speed (shown as "Patrol remounts" in the party speed tooltip), so they can run looters down.
  - Every patrol now raises its settlement's security: a castle's, or a town's without a Guard House, by +0.5 a day (the base game's figure for a patrol, without the Guard House's extra).
  - A settlement only raises a patrol once it can man the whole of it, can equip the men it has to recruit three times over (a castle once), and holds a month's keep for a small patrol (twice what it needs to keep one, so a new patrol is not stood down the next day); it stands its patrol down when it can no longer keep a small one for two weeks. A fief without a Guard House trains its first patrol in 10 days. Patrols are no longer handed out free when a new campaign starts; they are raised in the first weeks as settlements can afford them. The settlement's garrison stops recruiting before it eats into its patrol's money.
  - The clan and kingdom fief lists say "Cannot fund a patrol", "Too few men to raise a patrol" or "Saving to raise a patrol" instead of "No Guard House".
  - New settings in RBM Campaign (Settlement Upkeep): Settlement Patrols (on by default; off restores the base game's patrols) and Patrol Budget Share. The patrols' wages are paid through the spoils system, so with Troop Upgrade Cost at 0 the base game's patrols apply. New Patrol Logging setting (RBM Debug & Logging, off by default) writes the patrols to `logs/patrols`. Patrols already in a save simply fall under the new rules.
  - Switching Settlement Patrols or RBM Campaign off mid-campaign is safe: a castle's patrol, or one of a town without a Guard House, which the base game could not refill, is left alone until it is stood down the next day. Removing RBM altogether from a save that has such patrols is not safe.

### Auto-resolve
- **Troop perks now count in battle auto-resolve.** They used to work only in battles you fight, so auto-resolve misjudged troops that have them. Each perk uses its own value from the game, applied as it is in a fought battle:
  - Extra hit points (Well Built, Thick Hides, Unwavering Defense and the other hit point perks), also counted in the strength of parties on the campaign map. Extra horse hit points (Veterinary).
  - Extra arrows, bolts and throwing weapons (Deep Quivers, Fletcher, Resourceful, Well Prepared, Saddlebags for mounted troops).
  - More damage, by weapon type, on horseback, against horses, on head hits and against shields (such as Powerful, Blade Master, Clean Thrust, Strong Bows, Mounted Warrior, Horse Archer, Beast Slayer, Guards, Dead Aim, Shield Breaker). Like in battle, it is added after armor.
  - Ignoring part of the target's armor (Bodkin, Puncture, Weak Spot, Vandal, Chink in the Armor, Piercer).
  - Stronger horse charges (Full Speed), more armor on foot (Ignore Pain) and for armored horses (Tough Steed).
  - Less damage taken from missiles, from horse charges and on the shield (Skirmish Phase Master, Counter Fire, Skirmisher, Sure Footed, Steel Core Shields).
  - The War Sails perks, at sea only.
  - Perks for reload, draw, aim and weapon handling speed, accuracy, horse handling, Shield Wall and the perks that depend on how hurt the fighter is are not counted: auto-resolve has nothing they could change.
  - Follows the Troop Perks setting (and needs RBM Combat, like troop perks in battle) and auto-resolve's own perk setting.
- **Lords' and companions' own combat perks now count in auto-resolve too** (the same perks as above: damage, armor penetration, ammo, armor, damage taken). Until now only their hit points and their perks as captains counted. Needs auto-resolve's perk setting.
- **Extra hit points now protect against siege engines and very heavy blows in auto-resolve.** A ballista bolt, a fire pot and the cap on a single blow took the same share of every man's health, so a man with more hit points (from his perks, his commander's perks or a lord's own) was left just as close to death. They now do a fixed amount of damage, the same as before for an ordinary soldier or lord, so a tougher man has more left.

## v4.7.0 (changes since v4.6.0)

### Combat
- **Regular troops can now have hero perks.** Perks are given to a troop type in a new troop perks file (`ModuleData/rbm_troop_perks.xml`), and work in battle as they do for a hero. Only a perk's personal effect counts, and some perks do nothing for troops: they are only checked for heroes (such as Horse Master, Mounted Crossbowman, Projectile Deflection and Running Throw), or only act on the campaign map (most Charm, Trade and Roguery perks). About 150 perks do work for troops. Mighty Blow (Athletics) is only given to a troop with more than 250 Athletics, since below that its hit point bonus would take hit points away. A troop's perks are listed in its tooltip (hold Alt for what each one does) and as perk icons on its encyclopedia page. No troops have perks yet: this release only adds the system. It can be switched off with the new Troop Perks setting (Combat section, needs RBM Combat).
- New Troop Perk Logging setting (RBM Debug & Logging, off by default): writes a file per battle to `logs/troopperks` listing which troop perks the game actually checked for the troops that fought, and which it never checked.
- Shields, slings and sling stones can be found and made in different qualities again, as in vanilla: shields from Cracked (100 fewer hit points) and Battered up to Thick, Lordly and Legendary (450 more hit points), slings from Worn and Frayed up to Legendary, and sling stones from Chipped and Rough up to Legendary, including the Large Bag with more stones. RBM's versions of these items had lost their quality group, so they were always plain.
- Shield bashes and weapon bashes now reach about as far as a kick. They used to fall short at the same distance, so AI bashes often hit only air. The larger kick hit radius from v4.5.3 had never applied to weapon bashes, and the arm of a shield bash reaches less far forward than a kicking leg.
- A man knocked down by a kick or bash now falls where he stands instead of flying back as if hit by a charging horse. RBM now plays a short fall of its own instead of the game's knockdown: backwards when kicked from the front, forwards when kicked from behind. Kicks and bashes that do not knock him down still push him back as before.
- Thrown stones no longer hit a fast-charging rider twice as hard as a man standing still. A target running into a stone still takes more damage, but only up to about half as much again.
- Stone and chiseled sling bullets now do blunt damage, as in vanilla, instead of cut. Their damage against armor is unchanged. Hits on arms, legs and shoulders now count as blunt hits (a little more damage than a cut there), they deal a bit more damage to doors, siege engines and ships, and they wear down armor like other blunt weapons: cloth, leather and mail more slowly, plate faster. Lead sling bullets are still pierce.
- The combat settings file now has a blunt armor threshold for every weapon type (ExtraArmorThresholdFactorBlunt, default 5), and the existing blunt factor (ExtraBluntFactorBlunt) now works: it scales the blunt trauma of that weapon type. Both used to be fixed for every blunt weapon. Every weapon keeps its old damage. Armor wear from blunt hits now uses the same threshold, so maces, pommel and handle hits wear armor slightly more slowly than before. Thrown stones have their own row (Stone) and can now be tuned apart from maces. Your sling stone settings are carried over to their blunt values the first time you start this version.
- Form Fitting Armor (Athletics, armor 15% lighter) now counts everywhere RBM uses your armor's weight: the extra stamina heavy armor costs, the posture it adds, how hard it makes you to knock down with a kick or bash, and the load on your horse (its speed and charge weight). It used to lighten only the game's own movement penalty. Lighter armor costs less stamina and burdens the horse less, but also adds a little less posture and knockdown resistance.
- Percentage damage perks (and banner effects that raise or lower damage) are no longer lost on low-damage hits. The game rounds a hit's damage to a whole number after applying them, so a +5% perk on a 10-damage hit (10.5) did nothing, and against armor most hits are that small. The fraction now rounds up by chance (half the time for 10.5), so such a perk adds its full percentage on average. Hits without such a change deal the same damage as before.
- **Arrow Catcher (One Handed) does something again.** Its game effect, a larger shield area against missiles, has always been switched off by RBM, which keeps shields their real size. Now it makes you better at taking missiles on your shield: any missile blocked with your raised shield costs 25% less posture and stamina, does 20% less damage to the shield, and a pilum punching through it gets 15% less of its throw through (shallower, weaker wounds behind the shield). A formation captain with the perk gives his men half of that (not at sea), and the perk is halved at sea, as in the game. A shield on your back gets none of it. The posture part needs RBM Stance with posture on. Troops can be given the perk too. Its description in the game is updated to match.
- A hit taken on a shield (raised or on the back) now records which body part it struck, as in vanilla, so other mods and battle logs that look at blocked hits no longer see it as unknown.
- **Light polearms no longer thrust harder than heavy ones.** A thrust's force counted the wielder's whole body as moving at the speed of the weapon's point, and a lighter shaft has a faster point, so the lighter the weapon the harder it hit: a wooden pitchfork out-thrust every spear, menavlion and pike, and against heavy armor (where nothing gets through and the whole blow becomes blunt trauma) it hurt a man-at-arms more than a real polearm did. The body behind a thrust now moves no faster than with an ordinary weapon of its kind. A weapon lighter and quicker than that keeps only its own faster point. Pitchforks and other unusually light spears thrust up to about a quarter weaker (a two-handed pitchfork now just below a menavlion). Ordinary and heavy two-handed polearms, two-handed swords and two-handed maces are unchanged. Battle auto-resolve uses the same rule.
- One-handed thrusts counted the weapon's weight twice, once in the wielder's push and once in the weapon itself. It now counts once. One-handed thrusts (swords, daggers, one-handed spears, maces) are about 10–20% weaker for an average fighter, a little less for a highly skilled one, and heavy weapons used one-handed, such as lances, lose the most. Auto-resolve and the weapon tooltips show the same.
- Swings that land early or late in the arc, before or after full speed, were slowed down twice for it. They now are once, as thrusts already were. Such swings hit harder: one that used to land at four fifths of full speed now lands at nine tenths and hits with about a quarter more force, and at the slowest point a swing hits about twice as hard as before. Swings at full speed are unchanged, and so are auto-resolve and the weapon tooltips, which use a full swing.
- Thrown pilums now show and count their real armor-piercing in the weapon tooltip and in battle auto-resolve. In battle a pilum gets past armor three times as well as its damage factor alone, but the tooltip and auto-resolve left that out, so both showed it doing far less to armored men than it does. The tooltip's throwing speed for javelins, throwing axes and knives also now uses the same diminishing return on skill as the real throw, so it no longer shows a skilled thrower's throw faster than it is.
- Hits on the body were rounded down twice, before and after the extra or reduced damage for the body part hit. They now are once, after it, so hits on the head, chest, shoulders, arms and legs sometimes deal one more point of damage.
- Melee blows blocked with a shield still got the game's own weapon skill damage bonus, which RBM replaces with its own skill scaling and had already removed from hits on the body. Skilled fighters did more damage to shields than intended (roughly a tenth more for a one-handed fighter at 300 skill). Blocked blows now leave it out too, so shields last a little longer against skilled attackers. Missiles, kicks and bashes are unchanged.
- **Arrows, bolts and sling stones no longer tear through shields.** Against a shield they used the same armor threshold as against a man: once a shot was strong enough to get past the shield's armor, everything above that counted in full, so a strong bow could do 10 times as much to a thin shield as a slightly weaker one. A shot from a war bow could take 85–140 hit points off an ordinary shield, and a few of them broke it. Their damage to a shield (raised or on the back) now grows steadily with the strength of the shot, with no point where it jumps: about 5–20 per arrow or bolt, depending on the shot and the shield. Javelins, throwing axes and melee blows are unchanged. Battle auto-resolve already worked this way.
- Horse charges in the campaign no longer hit harder than in custom battles. Campaign battles counted an extra 100 kg on every charging horse, about a fifth more charge damage than the same horse and rider in a custom battle. Charges now count only the horse, its rider and what they carry, as in custom battles.
- **Blunt weapons do less trauma through armor.** A blow the armor stops still hurts: a share of what it stopped lands as blunt trauma, and against mail and plate that is almost all the damage any melee blow does. For blunt blows (maces, kicks, bashes, punches, pommel and haft hits, horse charges and falls) that share drops from 0.7 to 0.6, about 14% less. Cuts (0.25 for a sword) and thrusts (0.35 for a spear) keep theirs.
- **Armor material now matters for that trauma.** Mail is flexible and passes more of the impact on to the padding underneath (×1.2), while the rigid plates of lamellar, scale and coats of plates spread it out (×0.8). Cloth and leather are unchanged. This applies to every blow the armor stops, cut, thrust or blunt, but not to how easily a blow gets through. Against mail, swords, axes and spears now do about a fifth more and a mace about the same as before. Against plate they do about a fifth less, and a mace about 30% less, still the most of any of them. Mail still protects better than leather. Auto-resolve uses the same rules.
- The Blunt Trauma Multiplier setting (all trauma, any weapon) now defaults to 0.9 instead of 1, so every blow armor stops hurts a tenth less on top of the changes above. Your saved setting is set to 0.9 once, the first time you start this version; if you change it afterwards, your choice is kept.
- **Kicks, shield bashes and weapon bashes do less damage through armor.** A boot or a shield face spreads its force much wider than a mace head, but they used the same blunt trauma as a mace, so a kick hurt a man in heavy armor more than a sword cut did. Their trauma is now a quarter lower again: about half of what it was against plate, a third less against cloth and leather and a quarter less against mail. Since a hit's posture damage follows its damage, kicks and bashes also break posture a little less often.
- Sling stones are not part of the blunt cut: their own blunt factor is raised to make up for it, including in your saved settings the first time you start this version. The armor material changes their trauma like any other blow's.
- **A blow the armor stops now sounds like it hit armor.** The game plays a flesh-wound sound for any hit that does more than a little damage, and under RBM a blow stopped by mail or plate still does blunt trauma, often enough for that: a mace or axe on a fully armored man sounded like it cut him. A hit now plays the flesh sound only when the weapon actually gets through the armor; otherwise it plays the armor impact sound of the armor it lands on, however hard it hits. Hits that get through, and hits on unarmored men, sound as before.
- The stagger of an axe or two-handed weapon blocked without a shield (or on the wrong side) no longer adds a soft body-hit sound on top of the clash of the block.
- Posture breaks on a block, parry or chamber no longer add a body-hit sound on top of the clash of the block.
- The Armor Penetration Message setting now also reports blunt hits (maces, kicks, pommel hits). It used to show only cuts and thrusts.
- Mod compatibility: weapons from other mods of the low-grip polearm or pick type now deal damage like a one-handed spear and a mace. Their thrusts used to hit for about a twentieth of their strength, and their other blows ignored most armor. No weapon in the game or in RBM is of either type.
- **A man whose posture is broken by a hit on the body no longer stays broken.** Any other posture break gives him three quarters of his posture back at once (a third, if he broke himself by landing his own blow), but a break from a hit on the body left it at zero: every further hit broke him again, slowing time again for the player and unhorsing him again, and his next block was another break that let the blow through. He now gets his posture back as after any other break.
- Posture no longer regenerates during an exchange: it waits about a second after any posture loss. The faster regeneration out of combat now also waits until you haven't blocked, parried or been blocked for 10 seconds, not only until no one has hit you, so a man who only blocks no longer gets it in the middle of a duel.
- **Perfect parries are rewarded.** A timed weapon parry now costs a quarter of the posture the same blow would cost as a plain block (about 8 instead of 20 for a sword parrying a sword swing), and a timed shield parry half of what a shield block costs. A parry that leaves your posture standing also gives some back: 5% of your maximum for a weapon parry, 2.5% for a shield parry, so a clean weapon parry about pays for itself. A heavy enough blow can still break your posture through a parry. Through a weapon parry it then crushes through with half the force a broken block lets through; it never knocks your weapon or shield from your hand and never unhorses you. What the attacker loses is unchanged.
- **Chamber blocks are rewarded as they should be.** A chamber cost the defender more posture than a plain block (35 against 30 for a sword against a sword). It now costs him half what a perfect parry would (about 4) and gives him the weapon parry's 5% back, and the attacker whose blow is chambered loses half again as much as on a parry (about 52 instead of 35).
- **Landing a blow is nearly free for your posture.** The attacker now loses a fifth of the posture a landed hit used to cost him (5 instead of 25 for a sword swing). Its stamina cost is unchanged. Having your blow blocked or parried still costs the full amount.
- Skill matters less to posture. A recruit blocking a veteran used to lose about five times as much posture as the veteran blocking him; skill's effect is now the square root of what it was (about 2.3 times between the extremes), so the weapons involved count for more. Equal fighters are unchanged.
- Movement adds posture damage only when the two fighters close on each other. Any difference in their speed used to count, so a man stepping aside or backing away from a blow made both his block and the attacker's blocked blow cost more posture. Now only the speed at which they come together counts, and backing off as fast as the attacker comes adds nothing.
- **Missiles drain posture by the damage they do, like melee hits.** Every arrow drained the same posture (25, or 100 for a javelin or throwing axe), whether from a hunting bow or a war bow and whether it went through mail or glanced off plate. A missile now drains posture equal to the damage it does to you. So a strong bow or a javelin rocks you more, an arrow that barely gets through your armor hardly at all, and head hits drain more because they wound more. The stamina a missile costs follows the same amount.
- **Missiles blocked by your shield now cost posture and stamina.** They never did, not even with the old fixed amounts: shield blocks were never counted. A missile caught on a raised shield now drains a little posture: at most 2 for an arrow, bolt or sling stone and at most 6 for a javelin, throwing axe or throwing knife (less if it barely damages the shield; Arrow Catcher lowers it further). A long volley can still wear a shield man down. A shield on your back costs nothing. A missile that goes through the shield is only charged once.
- As before, only arrows, bolts and thrown weapons (javelins, throwing axes, throwing knives) cost posture when they hit you; sling stones only when they hit your shield. Thrown stones and siege engine shots never do.
- On Windows set to Turkish, shield blocks and parries cost the wrong posture, both to the man with the shield and to the attacker whose blow it stopped. A shield's posture costs were looked up by a name that Turkish spells with a different letter, so every shield got the base cost.
- Posture and stamina regenerated slower than intended, and the slower the lower your frame rate: about 3% slower at 60 fps and 10% at 20 fps. The stamina health regeneration came a little late for the same reason. All of them now run at the same speed at any frame rate.
- Breaking an enemy's posture in a chamber block, with your chamber or with the blow he chambers, now slows time like any other posture break you cause. The message you got when your own posture broke on his chamber, which always said 0 damage got through, is gone: nothing gets through, as after any other blocked blow.
- After a posture break, the posture bars now show the posture he gets back at once, instead of an empty bar for up to half a second.
- **Heavy weapons cost more posture to have blocked.** What the attacker loses when his blow is blocked or parried did not depend on the weapon's size: a two-handed sword cost the same as a dagger, a great axe or two-handed mace the same as its one-handed version. It now grows with the weapon: a dagger or short sword costs a little less than a sword (13 instead of 15 for a swing), a two-handed sword or two-handed polearm about 20, a two-handed axe or mace about 24. The posture the blocker loses is unchanged, so a heavy weapon still hits a guard hardest but is no longer free to swing into one.
- Daggers and short swords (seax, gladius, skaen, sica) drain a little less posture from the man who blocks them than a sword does (about 26 instead of 30 for a swing). They used to drain exactly as much.
- **Shield bashes, kicks and weapon bashes caught on a guard now rock the blocker.** One caught on a shield cost the man behind it almost nothing (5 for a large shield), so a bash could never open a shield wall unless it landed. It now costs about what a mace's overhead does: around 25 against a large shield, 30 against a small one, 40 against a weapon block. The kicker's own cost is unchanged.
- New Large Vertical Bars setting (RBM Stance, under Posture GUI, off by default): shows your posture and stamina as two big, thick vertical bars side by side at the right edge of the screen, with their current values underneath, instead of the small bars at the bottom right. Needs RBM Stance, the Posture System and Posture GUI on; the stamina bar shows only with the Stamina System on.
- **Melee weapon skill has stronger diminishing returns.** Each extra point of skill now adds less than the one before: melee damage at skill 100 is unchanged, a little higher below it, about 10% lower at 200, 15% at 250 and 20% at 330. This covers swing and thrust speed, the strength behind a thrust and the skill damage bonus. Skill's bonus to attack and parry animation speed follows the same curve. The weapon tooltips and battle auto-resolve follow. Thrown weapons, slings and bows are unchanged.

### AI
- With RBM Stance's posture system on, skilled AI fighters now parry more often instead of holding a block: up to 80% of the time without a shield (was 60%) and up to 65% with one (was 60%).
- **AI horse archers no longer bunch up and ride around into your other formations.** Since they started keeping formation (v4.5.4), they circled an enemy formation as one tight block and swung round its flank without regard for anything else on the field, so a line set up in steps led them straight past the next formation's archers and into its cavalry. Now:
  - They ride in a loose formation about as deep as it is wide, instead of a packed block, and re-form to keep that shape as they lose men.
  - When their circle would carry them past their target into another enemy formation, near enemy cavalry, or off the map, they turn and circle the other way. Formations backing up their target, such as archers behind it or on its wing, do not stop them circling in front of it. If both ways are blocked by a foot formation, they circle that formation instead. Only when that fails too do they ride to the nearest safe spot around their target and shoot from there. They back away only when they themselves are too close to danger, and otherwise hold where they are.
  - They judge this by where each enemy formation will be by the time they get there, from the direction and speed it is moving, so a formation marching into their path counts and one moving out of it does not. They also circle their target where it is heading, instead of trailing behind a formation on the march.
  - They fall back towards their own army only when enemy cavalry is riding at them at a charging pace and would reach them within about 4 seconds. Cavalry standing still, fighting a melee or riding across or away from them does not count.
  - How much they fear enemy cavalry and horse archers depends on how strong that formation is compared with their own. They keep further away from a stronger one and run from its charge earlier: twice as early, and twice as far, from one twice as strong. A much weaker one, about a third of their strength or less, they don't fear at all. They shoot its charge down instead of running and only keep the ordinary distance from it.
  - Skirmishing horse archers keep their bows and only draw a sword to defend themselves when an enemy is right on top of them (about 3 m). Most of them, all but those on lightly armored horses, used to charge any enemy that came within 20 m with the sword. Mounted javelin throwers skirmishing the same way now do the same instead of charging anything within about 8 m. Riders out of ammunition or unhorsed still fight.
  - With AI Behavior Logging on (RBM Debug & Logging), each change in what a horse archer formation is doing is written to the AI log as an `HA_ORBIT` line: circling, turning round, switching target, riding to a safe spot, backing off, holding, or falling back from cavalry.
  - At the map edge they turn round instead of riding up to the front of the enemy formation for several seconds.
- **Formations now shout.** Every formation on both sides gives voice to its orders, whether you are nearby or not:
  - When you give an order, several soldiers repeat it after you (more in a bigger formation), the men further away a moment later, and then the whole formation grunts as one. A second wave repeats the order a couple of seconds on, as it is passed back through the ranks.
  - The AI's captains call out their own orders (charge, halt, advance, fall back, retreat, move, follow and the formation changes), which they never did before; if the captain is down, a man of the front rank does it. His men echo and answer in the same way.
  - An army's main infantry formation (its biggest), moving in step, keeps up a massed marching call, the whole formation grunting as one (a real crowd recording, a little deeper for Sturgians and Nords and lighter for Khuzaits and Aserai; recorded by Shane Vincent at GameSoundCon 2024, CC0), in the rhythm of their captain's culture (with no captain, or when the captain is the army's commander or the player, the culture most of the men belong to, so a custom battle's Nord troops march as Nords whoever commands them): the Empire's called four even steps, Vlandia's two firm steps, Sturgia's slow heavy steps, Battania's loose lead and follow, the Aserai call and response (the captain, else the banner bearer, else a soldier calls the order, "Advance!" or the formation, "Shield wall!", and the formation answers), the Khuzait quick triplet and the Nords' single HUH every second and a half. Other troops keep a ragged single beat. That crowd grunt is the only grunt soldiers give: a halt that has settled, a shield wall, square or circle that has closed up, and a formation answering an order all grunt as one; single men only repeat orders and yell in a charge, they never grunt on their own.
  - A charge is loud: more than half of the formation yells as it starts (riders urge their horses on), together with the war cry, and half roars when the charging line first meets the enemy.
  - The crowd grunt carries up to 250 m, fading with distance and still heard from the right direction.
  - When the main infantry is not going anywhere but its men are still finding their places, its captain (else the banner bearer, else a soldier) keeps shouting the formation, "Form line!", "Shield wall!", every few seconds until it has closed up.
  - Horns: cavalry and horse archers blow a horn as they start a charge, just before their yells, a different horn for each culture (a deep viking horn for Sturgians, Nords and Battanians, a battle horn for the Empire and Vlandia, a ram's horn for Khuzaits and Aserai). Aserai and Nord formations of any kind also sound a horn with their orders. The horn is blown by the formation's man nearest the camera and carries much further than voices, up to 400 m. Two cavalry formations charging together sound one after the other. Recordings by Artiom_Constantinov, roemergruft, kirmm, Sauron974 and Sonicsheep (Freesound, CC0).
  - All of these added sounds (echoes, calls, grunts, the march, yells and roars, war cries, crowd answers and horns) come only from formations of 20 men or more; smaller groups sound as in the base game. The marching call needs a main infantry formation of more than 50.
  - Big formations sound like big formations. A formation of 20 or more that starts a charge gives its culture's war cry (its captain's, else its men's), the whole formation roaring (the game's cultural war cries, until now only heard in multiplayer warmup). AI orders, halts, closed shield walls and the first clash of a charge also get the game's crowd answer that until now only your own orders had.
  - Cooldowns per soldier and per formation and a limit on voices per second keep it from turning into noise. Sound only: nothing about how the troops fight changes. It can be switched off with the new Formation shouts setting (RBM AI section, needs RBM AI, on by default).
- New experimental Staggered order reaction setting (RBM AI section, needs RBM AI, off by default): when you give an order, each soldier starts to carry it out after his own short delay, between 0.3 and 2 seconds plus more the further he stands from you, how much more differing from man to man, and about one man in four is slow on the uptake and lags up to 2.5 seconds further (at most 6 seconds), instead of the whole formation moving at once. This also covers a new move position or direction, a halt and a formation change, where each man keeps heading for his old spot until his delay runs out. The AI's formations do the same when their commander changes their order (move, advance, fall back, halt, follow, or a new formation), measured from the formation's captain; the AI's constant small position updates are not delayed, and an AI formation is not held back again until its men have all moved off and at least 3 seconds have passed (the AI's quick back-and-forth orders, like its stepped advance, kept holding the men and cost performance). Charges and retreats are never held back.
- **AI infantry form shield walls again.** A soldier with a large shield counted towards a shield wall only if he had at most one throwing weapon left in his kit, so formations carrying javelins or pila (most infantry) never formed one until they had thrown them all, and with mods that give melee weapons hit points those were counted as ammunition, so no one counted at all. Now a soldier counts if he holds a large shield and is not holding a throwing weapon at that moment: a formation closes into a shield wall on the approach, opens when enough of its men take up their javelins to throw, and closes again when they are back on their spears. The share of shield-wall soldiers a formation needs is the new Shield wall share setting (RBM AI section, needs RBM AI, default 60%, was 70%); a shield wall opens again when the share drops 10% below it.
- AI soldiers of a charging formation who are running away no longer keep raising their shields for the fight, as in vanilla.
- RBM's AI no longer moves soldiers the game has taken out of their formation for a job, such as crewing a siege engine, nor the soldier a formation is ordered to follow, nor soldiers the game has found unable to reach their place. These now go where the game sends them, as in vanilla. Before, RBM's siege, cavalry, charge and ranged positioning could pull them away from it.
- **Posture, stamina, AI kicks and bashes and the combat slow motion now work with RBM AI turned off.** They have their own section in the RBM config, RBM Stance, with its own on/off switch (on by default) and the Posture System, Stamina System, Player Posture Multiplier, Posture GUI, AI Kick and Bash and Slow Motion in Combat settings under it, moved there from the RBM AI section. Your existing settings for them are kept. Turning RBM AI off no longer turns them off; turning RBM Stance off returns blocking, stamina and kicks to the base game and switches the slow motion off. The slow motion when you kill or knock out an enemy needs only RBM Stance; the one when you break a posture also needs the Posture System. AI Kick and Bash also works with the Posture System off: kicks and bashes still do their damage and can knock a man down, they just cost and take no posture or stamina. RBM AI's blocking and parrying still use the values tuned for the posture system whenever RBM Stance and the Posture System are on.

### Campaign
- **Villages no longer grow without end.** In the base game a village's hearths kept growing for the whole campaign, and in a long game villages reached thousands of hearths, with production and militia to match. Each village now has a land capacity of twice the hearths it started the campaign with, and its hearths close on it: a village grows by 0.5% of the room it has left each day, so it fills half the gap in under two years and slows as it nears the capacity, whatever its size. This replaces the base game's growth (+4, +1.2 or +0.2 a day by size). A village above its capacity (in an older save, or after a quest or event) loses 0.5% of the excess a day until it is back down to it, and a raided village recovers on the same curve. The bound town's or castle's Roads and Paths now raise the capacity of its villages by 5/10/15% instead of adding hearths a day, and the Irrigation project by another 15% while it runs. Raids, Grazing Rights, governor perks, the Empire bonus, a Merciful governor and quests still affect hearths as before. The village's map tooltip shows the capacity as "Land capacity", next to the settlement wealth, and the hearth breakdown shows the day's growth on the same line. Villages from other mods use their own starting hearths, so a mod that changes them changes the capacity too.
- **Village militia is capped at 10% of the village's hearths, rising to at most 20%.** Villages used the same militia caps as towns (40%, rising to at most 75%), so a large village could raise hundreds of militia and could no longer be raided. Towns and castles keep their caps. Kingdom policies that change militia still move a village's cap by 3 points each. A village with more militia than its new cap (in an older save) loses 5% of the excess a day, so oversized village militias break up over a few weeks instead of all at once.
- Deserter bands counted a village's militia twice when deciding whether they were strong enough to raid it, so they overrated every village's defence and left most of them alone. They now count it once.
- Building effects in the town and castle management screen are easier to read. RBM's effects are listed one per line, like the base game's, and each level shows only its own values, so the current and next level compare at a glance instead of repeating the same block of all three levels twice. Base-game lines for effects RBM replaces (castle Guard House militia, Farmlands food, Mason construction, Warehouse and Granary food limit) are no longer shown. The selected building's icon is much smaller, with tighter spacing around it, and the space goes to its description and effects. The description and effects are left-aligned, and the effects sit under "Now (level 1):" and "Next (level 2):" headers instead of the old "L-I :" tag on the first line. The Manage window is a little wider, and the extra room goes to the description column so its lines wrap less.
- Unticking a garrison's auto recruitment (party screen or clan screen) now stops it growing. RBM's garrison growth from the fief's treasury ignored the setting, so garrisons kept recruiting with it off, which also got in the way of mods that manage the garrison themselves (such as Improved Garrisons). With it off, a garrison its fief can no longer afford still shrinks as before. The garrison tooltip shows "Held — auto-recruitment disabled".
- Hovering a soldier's row on the party screen (either side) now shows his troop tooltip: tier, the experience he needs to upgrade, wage, maintenance, skills and any troop perks. The base game showed no tooltip there. On with the Troop Perks setting; a change to that setting reaches the party screen after a game restart.
- Wounded men you recover from a battle site now arrive with a few days' upkeep in their purse, as recruits do; only the men you actually take count.
- **Garrisons, militia and caravans now get the spoils a recruit brings, and so does every army a new campaign starts with.** A recruit arrives with 20 days of his upkeep in his purse. Until now only men recruited into a lord's party got it.
  - In a new campaign, every lord's starting army, garrison, militia and caravan begins with it, and so does your own army when you pick an advanced start (ruler, vassal, mercenary and the rest).
  - The rebel parties a town's rebellion raises bring it too, and take their arms off that town's market. What the market lacks they find elsewhere, at no cost to the townsfolk. Until now they came fully equipped from nowhere.
  - In play, garrison recruits, new militiamen, townsfolk who take up arms when their settlement is attacked, a new caravan's guards, mercenaries a caravan hires and men a sea convoy takes on in port all bring it too.
  - Garrisons and militia pay a quarter of a field soldier's upkeep (village militia a tenth), so they bring 20 days of that smaller amount.
  - A stack's purse is never filled past what its men would have brought, so militia that drift home and come back, or garrison men stood down and replaced, do not pile up extra spoils.
- AI lords now always appear at a town or castle, never at a village. A clan that holds any town or castle always appears at one of its own.
- An AI lord who gets a new party in play (after release or escape, or when his clan gains a party slot) no longer gets a ready-made army out of nowhere. He takes his starting men from the garrison of the town or castle he appears at, and recruits the rest:
  - at his own clan's town or castle, 40% of his party size, free;
  - at anyone else's, 20% of his party size, paid to that town or castle at the usual recruit price, as many as he can afford;
  - a king, 50% of his party size wherever he appears, free.
  - Lords at the start of a new campaign still begin with their armies, and minor-faction leaders (mercenary and outlaw bands with no fief to recruit from) still respawn with their band.
- When a lord's party leaves men in a castle or town garrison, or takes men from it, the men now bring their share of their troop's spoils with them, as they already did when you move troops yourself. Until now their share stayed behind with the men they left, and they arrived with nothing.
- Gear taken off a market to arm new men, or for a troop promotion paid in gold, is now chosen piece by piece at the market's own price, close to what each piece of the troop's kit is worth. When a piece costs more than the man needs, the difference is paid back to the town's citizens (shown in the Ledger as "Kit surplus returned"), so the town no longer loses its extra worth. For new men, such a piece also no longer uses up what the rest of his kit should get, and a piece the market can't supply or he can't afford no longer stops the rest of his kit being drawn. Cheaply armed militia now get cheaper gear in every slot instead of full-price gear in the first few. A new man's kit is now valued at his first battle set, the one he is actually armed in, rather than the average over all his sets. That lets him draw his whole kit instead of running out of money a few pieces in. A militiaman is priced the same way when a settlement checks whether it can afford to raise him, when a castle with no friendly town in reach pays to arm him, and when a village or castle is refunded for one it sends home.
- With War Sails, auto-resolved battles at sea now apply each captain perk as it works at sea. Perks that only work on land, such as the captain bonuses to your formation's weapon skills, no longer count in a sea battle.
- The RBM Ledger has a Clan finances tab: a record of what happened to your gold over the last 30 days. It shows your gold now, the net over the period and the average per day; a bar chart of each day's income, expenses, net (a losing day is a red bar) or gold; and a day-by-day table of income, expenses, net and closing gold, where hovering a number lists what made it up. A table by source lists the same lines as the gold tooltip (party wages, garrisons, workshops, fief income, kingdom and mercenary pay, troop maintenance and the rest) as they were actually paid each day, together with one-off gold as it happened (your cut of spoils, companions' share, mint cuts, promotions paid in gold, blood money, ship scrap, and, if you rule a kingdom, what you pay its mercenaries). Your own buying and selling has its own table, item by item, with units and gold each way. Whatever no line names (loot, quests, ransoms, gambling) is shown as Other, so each day's income minus its expenses is always its real change in gold. Gold paid to a companion leading his own party is noted but not counted, since it only reaches you later through party income. Tracking starts when you first load a game with this version. None of it affects the daily gold change or Expected Gold.
- Words about troops carousing now rise above a town or castle only now and then, about once every ten hours you watch it and at most twice a day, your own men's included. They used to appear nearly every hour in a busy town.
- The campaign map no longer stutters while RBM's campaign logs are switched on (spoils, economy, auto-resolve, caravan, troop power and garrison refill logging). The logs now keep their files open and write to disk once an in-game hour and before every save, instead of opening the file again for every line, and they no longer copy their lines into the game's own log. If the game crashes, up to the last in-game hour of log lines can be missing.
- The garrison refill log (`logs/garrison`) is now a setting, Garrison Refill Logging (RBM Debug & Logging, off by default). It used to be written whenever RBM Campaign was on.
- The campaign map runs faster with RBM Campaign on. Market prices, town food stocks, troop spoils and rations, and the arming of recruits, militia and upgraded troops do far less repeated work. Garrison and militia morale no longer computes vanilla's value only to replace it, supply caravan names are no longer rebuilt every frame, and the top bar's gold forecast no longer recalculates garrison wages twice. The AI's party strength estimates and auto-resolve battles reuse each commander's health perks instead of looking them up for every troop and every blow, the midnight town ledger snapshot no longer scans every town's records for each town, and the spoils bars on the party screen do less work each frame. Prices, purchases, battle results and every other result are the same as before, except that a leader's newly learned health perk can take up to an in-game hour to count.
- Auto-resolve now treats a thrown stone as the battle does: thrown at a fixed speed, instead of harder the more skilled the thrower.
- The auto-resolve battle panel shows the hardest hits, heroes' blows and siege engine shots of your battle again with the auto-resolve logging settings off. Since those settings moved to RBM Debug & Logging and became off by default, the panel only showed them while Detailed Auto Resolve Logging and Auto Resolve Per-Hit Detail were both on.
- Fixed: in auto-resolve, a siege assault could be thrown back at no cost to the defenders when the attackers had no siege engines standing. A castle held by a single man could rout an army of 800. Attackers now always have ladders against any stretch of wall without a siege tower or breach, as they do when you fight the assault yourself, so the storm is always fought.
- Deserter bands busy raiding a village look for new prey or villages at most every five in-game hours, instead of every hour while the raid lasts. They still drop a target the moment it is no longer valid and search again.
- The campaign map tooltip of a town, castle or village now shows its wealth only roughly, rounded to the first two digits (47,312 shows as ~47,000, 12,550 as ~13,000, 1,234,567 as ~1,200,000). Hold Alt for the exact amount. The construction lines in the same tooltip now have thousands separators too. RBM's lines (wealth, construction and recruit pool) now come right after the settlement's information (prosperity, loyalty and the rest) instead of at the very bottom, so they stay on screen when Alt lists a large garrison.
- Holding Alt over a town, castle or village on the campaign map now lists its troops and prisoners grouped by type and tier (such as "Infantry, tier 3: 45+3w") instead of one row for every troop type, so the tooltip of a large garrison no longer runs off the bottom of the screen. Heroes are still listed by name.
- **Parties led by your companions and family members can recruit better troops again, and so can AI lords who are not their clan's leader.** Under RBM's recruit prices a tier 3 recruit costs 5,000 or more away from your own fiefs, but the clan only ever topped these parties up to 5,000 gold, so they recruited almost nothing but tier 1 troops, a mercenary clan with no fiefs most of all. Now, for every clan, yours and the AI's:
  - the clan tops such a party up to 20,000 gold each day instead of 5,000 (paid from clan gold as before, so expect a larger party expense for a few days);
  - the clan takes back a tenth of the party's gold only above 40,000 instead of 10,000;
  - when the party recruits, it can borrow from the clan leader's gold, up to 20,000 a day, spending its own gold first. Whatever it does not spend goes straight back, and the clan leader always keeps at least 20,000.
- **AI clans now pay for their parties from a real daily budget, so their lords can fill their parties and form armies again.** How many men an AI lord may keep was set from his clan leader's gold alone, through fixed bands made for the game's own wages, a tenth of RBM's. Since the change above keeps much of a clan's gold in its other lords' parties and lends the leader's own gold out, clan leaders sat at about 20,000 gold, too poor on that scale to keep the 60% of their party size the game requires to form or join an army, and in a long-running campaign AI armies stopped forming altogether. Now each AI clan works out once a day what it can spend on its parties' wages: its steady daily income (fiefs, workshops, caravans, taxes and the rest, after garrisons and other upkeep, but before its parties' wages) plus a fifteenth of the gold it holds, counting the gold of all its lords' parties and keeping 20,000 back. That budget is shared out among its parties by their size, the clan leader's own party getting half again as much, and each party can keep as many men as its share pays for. Every party can keep at least a fifth of its full size even when the clan is broke. A clan that earns well or has gold saved fields big parties; a clan that is losing money or spending its savings sees its parties shrink over the following weeks. Even when its fiefs and garrisons lose money, a clan with savings always spends at least a thirtieth of them each day on its parties, so a rich clan with costly garrisons no longer fields nothing. Your own clan's parties are not affected, and mercenary companies still have no wage limit.
- AI lords need a smaller combined army strength to raise an army: 700 instead of the game's 1000, matched to how RBM rates troop strength (an ordinary lord's men count for a little less than in the base game, so armies of four or five lords fell just short). Your own armies are not affected.
- **AI army leaders can call more lords, and hire mercenary companies from farther away.** An AI lord only raises an army to besiege a castle or town, and RBM only lets him start a siege when his army is about two and a half times as strong as the defenders, so the armies AI lords raised (most just 3 to 6 lords) rarely set out. A kingdom's armies together may now hold up to 80% of its lords' parties instead of 70%. Who can be called is unchanged: a lord within the usual call range (8 times the distance between two neighbouring towns) with more than 15 days of food and more than 60% of his party size, and the leader still pays influence for each lord he calls. An AI army leader now looks for a mercenary company to hire from half as far again (12 times that distance).
- **AI lords now carry more food, so they can raise and join armies again.** A lord's men bought 20 days of food and bought no more until it was all eaten, so on average a lord had only 10 to 13 days of food left. That often failed the game's checks for forming an army (more than 12 days for the leader, more than 15 for each lord he calls). Now AI lords' men buy 30 days of food, and at any town or village they stop in they top it back up once it falls under 20 days, paying only for the days they are short. The food is paid from the men's spoils as before and costs the same per day, about 12 gold a man at average prices, so about 1,800 a day for a party of 150. A party pays for about 10 more days once, the first time it tops up, and its men now eat less of the food bought with their lord's gold. Parties led by your companions and mercenary companies count as lords' parties. Your own party and caravans, villagers and garrisons are not affected.
- AI lords no longer sell off pack animals and spare horses they still need to carry their load. When a town could not afford all of a lord's loot, the rest stayed in his baggage, but he still sold his surplus animals, and the game then threw away whatever he was now too overloaded to carry. A lord now keeps enough animals to carry everything he has. Lords also buy spare horses for their foot soldiers again: pack animals used to take the whole budget for animals on each visit, so few lords ever bought a spare horse. When a lord is short of both, pack animals now get half of that budget and spare horses the rest.
- New option **Party Budget Logging** (RBM Debug & Logging, off by default) writes each AI clan's daily party budget, and why each clan leader could or could not raise an army, to `logs/partybudget`.
- New option **Stuck Party Logging** (RBM Debug & Logging, off by default, needs RBM Campaign) writes to `logs/stuckparties` every party on the campaign map (lords, caravans, villagers, bandits, fleets) that has somewhere to go but has not moved for a day: where it is and the nearest settlement, where it is heading and why, whether it is at sea, heading for a port or has ships, and whether the game could find a path there. A second line follows when it moves again. For reporting parties that get stuck near coasts and ports.
- New debug console command `campaign.rbm_test_battle [countPerTroop] [attacker=<groups>] [defender=<groups>] [player=attacker|defender]` (default 5, needs RBM Campaign): starts a field battle at a random spot on the campaign map where both sides have the same army, that many men of every culture's regular troops (recruit and noble lines), every tavern mercenary and every minor-faction troop, so every troop type can be tested in one fight, by hand or with auto-resolve. `attacker=`/`defender=` give a side only some groups (a culture id such as `vlandia`, `mercenaries`, `minor`, or a minor-faction clan id; several joined by commas, e.g. `attacker=vlandia defender=sturgia,mercenaries`), and `player=defender` puts you on the defending side. Run without parameters it opens a setup window instead: Default (every troop type, you attack) asks only for the men per troop type, and Custom also lets you pick each side's cultures, mercenaries and minor factions and your side, then save those picks as a named preset. Like custom battle presets, each one is a file in `Documents\Mount and Blade II Bannerlord\Configs\RBM\TestBattlePresets\`; the window's first page can load a preset, rerun the last used setup or delete presets. Your own troops, prisoners, inventory, gold, renown, morale, wounds, troop spoils and map position come back when the encounter ends, whether you win, lose, retreat, leave or are captured. Experience your heroes gain in it is kept.
- **Neutral mercenary companies.** Each main culture now has two mercenary companies (War Sails adds two Nord ones), each a band of its own with one captain, appearing in their home towns the first time you load a game with this version. They never join a kingdom on their own. Between contracts they hire tavern mercenaries when short of men, hunt bandits they can beat, and otherwise patrol around their home town.
  - A company is made only of mercenary troops, the kind taverns hire out: it starts with them, hires only them in taverns, takes no village or town volunteers, recruits only mercenaries from its prisoners, and its men upgrade only into other mercenary troops.
  - An army leader can hire a company for the length of his army: a fee of ten days of the company's wages up front, then one and a half times its wages each day. The party leading the army pays, from its own gold first and then from its clan's (an AI clan leader always keeps 5,000); if leadership of the army passes to another party, that party pays from then on. When the army breaks up, the company is sent away, or the army's leader and his clan can no longer pay, the contract ends and the company goes back to being neutral. It costs no influence.
  - Between contracts a company pays its men half wages, and they bank half in their spoils; under contract they are paid in full. The hire fee and the daily pay are always reckoned on the full wages. Men never desert a company because its wage bill is over its limit (an unpaid company still loses morale), a company keeps five days of wages in hand when hiring tavern mercenaries and only heads for a tavern it can afford, and its captain does not buy lordly equipment, horses or pack animals. A company promotes only the men their own spoils fully pay for: its captain's gold never buys a promotion's kit (one tier 5 kit could cost him everything he had).
  - What you pay your hired companies now shows in your daily gold change, the Clan screen's Finances and the RBM Ledger as one line per company ("Mercenary company: The Wolves of the Glen"), and is taken with the rest of your daily finances. A hire fee shows in the Ledger as "Mercenary company hire fees". The army management screen shows each company's daily price next to its hire price.
  - AI lords hire up to two nearby companies when they gather an army, if their party and clan together can afford them and still keep 5,000. A company is a big expense, so a lord may spend most of his and his clan's gold on one.
  - You can hire them from the army management screen when you lead or gather an army: idle companies appear in the party list with their price in gold under their name, and the cart shows the gold the hired companies will cost against your gold. You cannot confirm if you cannot pay. The daily fee comes out of your gold.
  - A company whose party is destroyed returns with a new band a week later, once its captain is free. A company whose captain is killed is replaced by a new one a week later. New and returning bands always appear at a town that is safe for them (not under siege, not at war with them), the company's home town if it is. If a band cannot appear, it tries again the next day instead of waiting another week.
  - An idle company that has dwindled to its captain and a few men (under 15% of its size limit) with too little gold to hire anyone (under 5,000) no longer stays stuck that way: after a week like that, its captain raises a new band at a safe town, as if his party had been destroyed, and keeps the men, prisoners and goods he still had.
  - A hired company whose party is destroyed and whose captain is taken prisoner by an enemy of the kingdom that hired it now stays under contract (unpaid) until he is free again. It used to leave the kingdom at once, which made peace between the company and his captors, so he was set free for nothing, even from your dungeon.
  - The base game's "serve my kingdom as mercenary" offer is not available for these companies. New setting Mercenary Companies (RBM Campaign, on by default) turns all of it off. Switching it off in a running campaign quietly disbands the companies already there within an in-game hour (a company in a battle or siege waits for it to end; a hired one leaves its kingdom first), and switching it back on raises new ones. The disbanding only runs while RBM Campaign is on: to remove RBM Campaign from a campaign with companies, switch Mercenary Companies off, play an hour, then turn RBM Campaign off.
  - New setting Mercenary Companies Logging (RBM Debug & Logging, off by default) writes the companies' activity to `logs/mercenaries`: companies created, respawned and destroyed, where they head and what they hire, every contract from hire through daily pay to its end and the reason, and which companies an AI army passed over and why.

A caravan heading home from an island port to a town without a port now sails to the nearest coast and walks the rest instead of standing at the gate looking for a road. A caravan from an inland town that crosses by sea no longer waits two days on the shore for its ship: its ship now waits at the nearest port and only has the short sail from there. Any supply caravan that does not move for 12 hours is given a different route, up to twice, and if it still cannot move, its trip is settled at its home town as if it had come back (its takings paid, its unsold goods returned) and it disbands. Caravans already stuck in a save are freed this way within about a day and a half. With Caravan Logging on, these show as `STUCK` and `STRANDED` lines.
- Parties on the campaign map now cross bridges at full speed. The game slowed a party on a bridge by 30%, the same as wading through a ford, so a long bridge was slower to cross than the ford beside it. Fords and shallow water still slow parties as before.
- **Detailed Auto Resolve now works with RBM Campaign turned off.** It has its own section in the RBM config, RBM Auto Resolve, with Auto Resolve Routing and Auto Resolve Perks under it, and is on by default as before. Turning RBM Campaign off no longer turns it off with it; only the Detailed Auto Resolve setting does. Your existing settings carry over. Detailed Auto Resolve Logging now needs Detailed Auto Resolve on instead of RBM Campaign, and the auto-resolve battle panel's title no longer says RBM Campaign.
- Fixed: with RBM Combat off, archers, crossbowmen and horse archers did almost no damage in Detailed Auto Resolve. Their shots were priced on the arrow's or bolt's own damage alone, but with RBM Combat off the game's own items put nearly all of a shot's damage on the bow or crossbow (a woodland longbow 64, its bodkin arrows 3), so a Battanian Fian needed several hundred arrows to bring down a Vlandian Sergeant, and archers using arrows with no damage of their own did not shoot at all. Shots now count the bow's or crossbow's damage plus the arrow's, as a fought battle does, less what the arrow loses in flight: a Fian now brings a Sergeant down with about 14 arrows that hit him (misses and shield blocks not counted), and a Vlandian Sharpshooter with about 9 bolts. With RBM Combat on nothing changes.
- With RBM Combat off, Detailed Auto Resolve now follows the game's own rules without RBM in more places. With RBM Combat on nothing changes.
  - Hits on the head, body, arms and legs count as the game counts them (head 1.2 times, or twice for an arrow or bolt, legs 0.8 times), instead of RBM Combat's values (head 1.5 times, arms and legs about half). Arrows and bolts do about a quarter more damage than before this, melee blows about a tenth more.
  - A soldier's weapon skill raises his damage by the game's own amount for that weapon: per skill point 0.15% for one-handed, 0.16% for two-handed, 0.07% for polearms (lances included), 0.11% for bows, 0.06% for throwing weapons and slings, and nothing for crossbows. It used to be the same for every weapon, up to 30% at 250 skill. Skilled crossbowmen now do about a fifth less damage, spearmen and lancers about a tenth less, swordsmen a little more.
  - A horse's charge weighs as much as the same horse's charge with RBM Combat on. The game's own horses list about twice RBM's charge value, so with RBM Combat off charges hit about a third harder than intended (a Vlandian T3 warhorse added 64% to the rider's blow, now 29%), and so did a horse running onto a braced spear.
  - Horses no longer hurt themselves by charging. Without RBM the game never damages a horse for charging, but auto-resolve took 8 to 25 hit points off a horse for every charge (the most onto braced spears), so cavalry lost their horses faster than in a fought battle. They are now hurt only by the blows that hit them.
  - The AI's party strength is the game's own. Equipment Based Troop Power (RBM Campaign) now needs RBM Combat on as well: it prices troops by RBM's combat rules and items, and with RBM Combat off it misread the game's own items (bows were valued by their missile speed as if it were RBM's draw weight). With RBM Combat off, the encounter screen's strength, the AI's attack, flee and siege decisions and the army strength floor (back to the game's 1000) all use the game's own strength.

## v4.5.4 (changes since v4.5.3)

### Combat
- Ranged weapons from other mods whose item usage RBM doesn't know now fly at their own missile speed instead of 10 m/s, which made some of them useless. Vanilla light crossbows are treated as crossbows.
- Pommel and handle hits no longer get through armor better on a sharper blade or a Fine/Masterwork weapon. The blade never touches the target, so its sharpness and the item modifier no longer count.
- Swings with weapons that aren't built from crafting parts (torches, banners, many weapons from other mods) now do posture damage when blocked or parried. They used to do none.
- Pommel and handle hits now hit as hard as the swing behind them. A graze with the hilt used to do as much damage as a full pommel strike, whatever the weapon weighed; a heavier weapon and a fuller swing now hit harder, and skill still adds to it. Even an unskilled man's pommel hit keeps a small minimum. Torches, whips, push forks, banners and maces are unchanged.
- Pommel and handle hits on a shield no longer damage it as if the blade had struck: they get no edge bonus against shields (including the extra shield damage of axes and shield-breaking weapons) and strike the board as a blunt blow.
- Weapons that aren't built from crafting parts (many weapons from other mods) can now land pommel and handle hits: a swing that connects with the tenth of a sword or dagger nearest the hand, or below the top third or so of an axe, mace or polearm, is a handle hit. It used to count as a full blade hit.
- Perks that add head armor (such as Metallurgy) now also protect the face, and perks that add arm armor now also protect the gap under the shoulder. Face and under-shoulder hits used to count only the armor pieces themselves and ignore these perks.
- Swings count as face hits less often: besides coming in head-on, a swing now has to land on the face itself, not on the cheek, temple or brow, which the helmet covers. Thrusts and missiles are unchanged. With the battle hit log on, every head hit now also records where it landed on the head and whether it counted as a face hit.

### AI
- **Kick and bash knockdowns are no longer certain on a second kick:** a kick or bash now always knocks a man down only when it empties his posture, or while he is still reeling from a posture break or posture tiredness. A second kick or bash within about a second only makes a knockdown more likely (×1.5). A man who is already down is not kicked at and not knocked down again, and a kick or bash from a friend gets no RBM knockdown.
- AI soldiers now see an enemy whose posture is broken or nearly broken (25% or less) as an opening for a kick or bash. A kick or bash that just landed no longer counts as one.
- AI banner bearers no longer kick their own formation. AI soldiers now only kick or bash an enemy, and not while a friend stands within reach in front of them, where the kick could land on him instead.
- **Flank cavalry stays organized before the lines meet, and covers its infantry:** cavalry guarding a flank charges enemy cavalry within about 70 m of its post, or any enemy within about 35 m. It also charges enemy cavalry within about 70 m, or horse archers within about 90 m, of any friendly foot formation, at any point along its line. When the other flank has a guard, it leaves anything on that side of the main line to it. Arrow fire alone no longer draws it out, and it no longer charges distant enemy infantry. A charge is called off after 25 seconds, or when the enemy pulls away (for a charge to cover infantry, once the enemy is about 110 m clear of it). It is also called off when the riders scatter or get about 60 m from their post (or about 50 m beyond the infantry they are covering). The riders then regroup at the post and wait 5 seconds before charging again.
- **Cavalry charges ride through and hit what they charge:**
  - A charge that bogs down in melee now pulls out 8 seconds after the riders actually start trading blows. The clock used to start some 25-30 m short of a wide enemy line, so riders broke off after only a few seconds of fighting.
  - Riders pulling out of a charge now ride on through, to a point beyond the enemy along the line they charged in on. They used to turn back the way they came when they hadn't got through.
  - A formation put back on the charge after doing something else starts a fresh charge instead of breaking off at once.
  - Riders now aim at the formation their formation is charging, not at whatever enemy stands nearest, such as skirmishers in front or the next block over. On the way in they steer a little toward the middle of that formation and lead it when it moves, instead of riding at its near corner and sliding down its side.
  - Charging cavalry now chooses what to charge by type, distance and strength. Enemy cavalry is the preferred target, then horse archers, then archers, then infantry. A stronger formation is worth a somewhat longer ride and a handful of stragglers is not. Infantry and archers that have their back to the cavalry, or their side, are more likely to be charged. Cavalry used to charge enemy infantry whenever any was on the field, even with archers much closer. The target is chosen when each charge starts and kept until the charge ends.
- **Horse archers keep formation:** AI horse archers ride and shoot in formation as it circles the enemy, instead of every rider breaking off to circle his own target. A rider only leaves his place to get away from an enemy within about 15 m, and draws his sword within about 5 m. The formation also no longer switches between enemy formations unless another one is clearly closer.
- **Attackers keep advancing:** in field battles the attacking side could stop and stand in place after a reinforcement wave or a change of tactic. Its formations now regroup for at most 10 seconds at a time while advancing. While its archers shoot, its infantry keeps walking forward (6 m every 4 seconds) instead of holding, and without usable archers it simply advances. Enemy cavalry more than 150 m away no longer makes it stop and brace.
- The rally after reinforcements now lasts at most 45 seconds and can't start again for 30 seconds. Men still spawning or stuck on terrain no longer keep a formation standing where the wave entered.
- Soldiers who swam into deep water no longer stand frozen there after a charge order.
- With the infantry split tactics, both flank infantry formations now charge when the battle is joined. When any formation emptied during the advance (for example a few horse archers who lost their horses), one flank could drop out of the tactic and stand guarding its flank for the rest of the battle.
- Rear ranks of a charging infantry line follow the men in front instead of stepping back or sideways. A friend ahead who is still moving toward the enemy now counts as only a quarter of an obstacle; once he stops to fight he counts in full again.
- When a soldier in a charging line steps back, sideways or toward a friend, he now judges the spot by where the men around him, friend or enemy, will be half a second from now. He steps into a gap a man is just leaving, and not into the path of a man walking toward that spot.
- In first person, posture tiredness now staggers the player backward, so the camera no longer looks through your own body. AI soldiers and third-person players keep the forward stagger.
- A rider whose posture is broken by a heavy enough blow (a third of his posture or more) is now actually knocked off his horse. The dismount was there but never happened, unless a crush-through also dealt damage.
- Kicks, shield bashes and pommel strikes that hit a block now drain the blocker's posture as unarmed attacks, as intended, instead of as a swing of the weapon in hand. A kick from a man holding a two-handed axe no longer drains a blocker like an axe blow.
- A kick or bash now costs the kicker only its cost when he starts it (20 posture and 50 stamina, less with skill), wherever it lands. A kick that landed used to cost him posture and stamina a second time.
- Unarmed overhead attacks now cost as much posture as unarmed swings and thrusts.
- **Crush-through damage now comes from the blow that was blocked:** when a weapon block or parry breaks posture, the damage that gets through is what that strike would have dealt had it landed, at ×0.85. It used to be a perfect full-speed swing against the defender's armor averaged over his whole body, so a sloppy or late swing that broke a guard hurt as much as a perfect one, and more than the same swing landing clean on a helmet or cuirass. The strike's speed, where it met the guard on the weapon, and the attacker's movement now count. The body part it would have hit is judged by how high the weapons met (and, at shoulder height, how far to his side, for a shoulder hit), not taken from the guarding arm the game reports for a block, and that part's armor and damage multiplier apply, as do stamina and handle/pommel hits. The crush-through now also lands on that body part, so the hit reaction and the hit log show it there instead of on the arm, and the crush-through message names it. It is also reported with the damage type it was dealt with: blunt for a handle or pommel, cut for a sword thrust that met the guard off the tip.
- Aserai Vanguard Faris no longer stand in place on a charge order. RBM forced their lance back into their hands whenever they drew a javelin more than 25 m from their target, so they kept swapping weapons and never charged. Riders now keep a drawn javelin at any range beyond 6 m, and still charge with the lance in both hands.

### Campaign
- Clothing, armor and horse armor workshops make twice as many items from the same materials.
- The nine gambeson horse armor recipes (artisans, linen, wool and tannery) produced nothing while still using up labor and leather, linen or felt. They now make horse armor.
- Wool, hides and cattle are cheaper (wool 100 → 80, hides 70 → 60, cows 480 → 380). Tanning now pays in towns where hides cost up to about 1.6 times their value (was about 1.4).
- Supply caravans carry up to 10 goods (was 6), and part of each caravan is kept for workshop materials, so food and staples can no longer crowd them out. Food is still sold first on arrival.
- Cattle and sheep farms show the cow or sheep icon on the map and in the ledger instead of butter or wool.

  - The 14-day averages of your spoils cut, companions' spoils share, mint revenue and gold-paid promotions are no longer counted as daily income or expenses. That gold is paid when the battle or minting happens.
  - Garrison maintenance your fiefs can't pay is now charged once a day and shows in the Daily Gold Change message. The expected change shows tomorrow's charge, where it used to show only what had been paid since midnight.
  - The settlement wealth tax line shows the next day's tax instead of yesterday's, which ran high while a rich town was paying down its savings.
  - Your workshops that are low on capital are no longer charged their daily expense twice.

## v4.5.3 (changes since v4.5.2)

### Combat
- **New shields:** four Khuzait kalkan-style strapped shields and a Cataphract Targe, worn strapped to the arm by Khuzait and Imperial cavalry. They swap back to their hand-held versions when the passive shoulder shields option is off.

- Spiked mace thrusts no longer pierce armor better than swords (mace pierce threshold 2 → 4). Existing configs still on the old default are updated once.
- Kicks, shield bashes and weapon bashes deal real blunt damage instead of 1–2 points, using the same model as punches: it scales with skill, boot, shield or weapon weight and the body part hit, and armor reduces it. A bash blocked by a shield and a kick into a raised shield do no damage. Kicks and bashes also connect more easily (larger hit radius). The damage follows the new "AI Kick and Bash" setting.
- Spears carried on the back no longer hang low and sink into the ground.
- Armor worn down in battle no longer stays damaged on the saved gear after the War Sails storyline battle, stealth missions or closing the inventory mid-mission.
- Sheathing a bow with an arrow nocked no longer leaves the string drawn, and a dropped bow no longer carries a phantom arrow.
- Village livestock no longer get knock-downs or 0-damage hit reactions.
- The tier-5 Kama dagger blade is no longer unlocked at campaign start.
- The Battanian Oathsworn now carries a sword.
- **Metal shields stop missiles:** nothing goes through a metal shield, raised or on the back. Missiles bounce off it, and arrows and bolts break 80% of the time. The shield still takes the hit's damage.
- Arrows and bolts that hit a wooden shield stick 80% of the time, break 15% and bounce off 5%. Arrows and bolts that don't get through body armor (blunt trauma only) break or glance off instead of sticking.
- **Pila punch through shields:** a thrown pilum that hits a raised wooden shield sticks in it and can drive through to wound the man behind, keeping 90% of the throw's force minus 5% per point of shield armor. Thrown pila no longer deal double damage to shields, and the javelin body-damage factor is halved.
- **Stuck missiles fall out:** javelins, throwing axes and throwing knives stuck in a shield or a living body work loose after about 5 s. Arrows and bolts fall out of shields after about 45 s (at most 8 per shield), and a body holds at most 4 arrows. Each drops as a loose item you can pick up. Two new RBM Combat sliders set the times; 0 keeps vanilla behavior.
- **Flying arrow thickness (experimental):** a new RBM Combat slider (1–10, default 1) makes arrows and bolts thicker in flight with Better Arrow Visuals, which are true to size and hard to follow. Arrows in the quiver, on the string and stuck ones keep their real size, and the hit test is unchanged.
- **Armor sliders:** a new Armor Effectiveness slider in the RBM Combat options (0.25–3, default 1) scales every armor value, for all weapons. It also feeds auto-resolve and troop power, and does not change how fast armor wears. A collapsible "Advanced Armor" group below it holds three finer sliders that work on top of it: Armor Multiplier (0.5–4, default 2, how strongly armor absorbs blunt trauma), Blunt Trauma Multiplier (0–3, default 1) and Armor Penetration Threshold (0–3, default 1, how much of a blow armor stops outright).
- Auto-resolve follows the live armor rules: thrusts and arrows no longer get a partial-penetration floor that live combat removed.
- The Pavise and Deployable Pavise shields have more armor (7 → 10).

### Ranged / AI shooting
- AI arrows and bolts no longer fall short at long range. RBM's arrow drag didn't match the engine's, so the AI aimed with one drag value while missiles flew with another.
- AI archers, crossbowmen and slingers hold fire until the target is actually within reach, instead of opening fire at 200–300 m and landing tens of metres short. AI javelin and throwing-axe throwers do the same, instead of throwing at targets 30 m and more away and landing well short.
- AI javelins and throwing axes go where the AI aims them. RBM used to pitch AI throws down after the AI had aimed (throwing axes by 5°, javelins when the target moved away, and all of them when there was no target), which only made them land short.
- Javelins, throwing axes and throwing knives use the game's own air drag again, so axes and knives carry a little further and hit a little harder.
- AI slingers aim at the target instead of over it.
- AI shooters no longer over-lead moving targets, which makes them noticeably better against walking infantry and charging cavalry.
- Skilled AI crossbowmen are at least as accurate as vanilla ones and settle their aim as fast.
- AI shooters use the correct launch speed after re-equipping or picking up a bow, crossbow or sling.
- **Ranged aim arc (experimental, off by default):** a new RBM Combat setting for the player. While you draw a bow or crossbow, wind up a sling or ready a javelin, throwing axe, throwing knife or stone, a dotted arc shows the missile's predicted flight, with a marker where it will land (red on an enemy, green on a friendly) and a ring on the ground showing how far your current aiming error can scatter the shot. In third person, aiming upward also lifts the camera and tilts it down so the landing point of a high shot stays on screen. Your aim is unchanged; the crosshair is hidden while the camera is moved. Throws include your own running speed. The camera frames where the shot lands on the ground, so sweeping the aim across walls, ladders and towers in a siege no longer makes it jump.

### AI
- Sieges: attackers on the walls fight instead of idling until the gate opens. Attackers no longer stand shuffling outside a breached wall, and troops leave ladders and towers the AI has given up on.
- Cavalry ordered to dismount now dismounts and holds its formation, including after the formation is delegated to the AI.
- Retreating riders are no longer held back by RBM's speed limit.
- Archers no longer walk into the enemy to pick up spent arrows. Ammo lying within 25 m of an enemy (45 m for horsemen) is ignored.
- AI missile troops only look for spent ammo when they are nearly out: below 15% of what they can carry of that type (vanilla: any quiver at half), or down to their last javelin, throwing axe or throwing knife. They also no longer break off drawing, shooting or reloading to fetch it.
- Troops that lose their melee weapon pick up a dropped one again.
- Polearm troops no longer switch weapons back and forth while an enemy hovers at the edge of range.
- Swimmers in field battles (e.g. on river maps) are no longer treated as being on land, so they are no longer ordered to cheer or to pick up weapons while swimming.
- **Rally after reinforcements:** when part of an AI infantry formation is stranded far ahead of or behind the rest (typically first-wave survivors standing in front of a fresh wave), the formation stops and gathers before advancing again. After a reinforcement wave it gathers up to 200 m in front of where the wave entered, but never within 200 m of the enemy. Stragglers run straight to the nearest free place in the line at full speed. Works for both sides and every field tactic until the lines meet.
- Advancing AI lines no longer leave a few men lagging far behind. When the line widens, each man takes the nearest place in it instead of one on the far flank.
- Advancing AI lines that are already in formation move at their slower men's full speed instead of being held back further. The formation's speed limit now applies only while the line is ragged.
- Siege attackers whose only melee weapon is two-handed (e.g. the Imperial Flame line) no longer sheathe it to raise their shield and fight bare-handed.
- **AI kick and bash (new RBM AI setting, on by default):** AI foot soldiers now kick, shield bash and weapon bash, which the vanilla AI never does. They try it at close range against an enemy who is blocking, holding a weapon ready, showing his back or staggered; a clearly more skilled fighter also tries it at other times. How often depends on the two fighters' relative skill (mostly Athletics, partly weapon skill). A raised shield stops a bash but not a kick, so the AI kicks a man behind a raised shield and otherwise picks either. With the posture system on, throwing one costs posture and stamina, hit or miss.
- **Kick and bash knockdowns (same setting, player and AI):** a kick or bash always knocks down an enemy who is already staggered (posture break, posture tiredness or another kick or bash a moment earlier) or whose posture it empties. Otherwise it has a chance based on relative skill against an enemy holding a weapon ready or hit from behind; it is higher for a kick than a bash, from behind and against a tired enemy, and lower the heavier his armor.
- AI soldiers never kick or bash while bracing a polearm.
- Posture tiredness plays a new stagger animation instead of the pick-up animation, and input can no longer cancel it.
- Fewer stutters near the enemy: an advancing AI line re-forms at most once every 10 s, and formations no longer flip between line, loose and shield wall when they sit right at the switch point.
- **The bigger army fields more men:** in field battles the battle size is no longer split 50/50. Each side's share follows the square root of its men left, so a bigger army has more men on the field than the enemy but less than its full advantage: 2:1 gives 59:41, 3:1 gives 63:37, 5:1 gives 69:31. Neither side ever gets more than 75% or less than 25%. A small army that fits in half the battle size still brings everyone, as before, and that guarantee fades smoothly for armies a little larger (5000 vs 600 starts 600 vs 400). The share is recalculated from the men each side has left at every wave, so it shifts as armies take losses.
- **Reinforcement waves refill fully:** every wave in a field battle now brings its side back up to its share of your battle size setting (also with battle-size mods), as long as it has men left in reserve. In 4.5.2 the side that called the wave got a fixed half-line, while the side pulled in with it was topped up to full, so the side already losing kept facing a fresh enemy line. A wave held up because the game had no room to spawn it now also covers the men lost while it waited. The other side is topped up once when a new wave starts, instead of every few seconds while a slow wave is still coming in.
- **Better frame rate when AI formations charge:** the frontline fighting system (men stepping forward, back and sideways in a melee line) now sizes up its surroundings only when a soldier makes a new decision, about once a second, instead of every frame. Charging soldiers also find their targets more cheaply, and AI formations no longer briefly take on charge, shield wall or line settings from a behavior they then don't pick, which caused short stutters. Battles play the same.
- **Better frame rate with cavalry:** AI riders no longer search a wide area around themselves for enemies every frame. They only look when an enemy formation is close, and then a few times a second. Charging riders share one picture of the enemy's positions instead of each building their own, and riders pushing through crowds, lancers choosing between lance and sword, horse archers judging their reach and troops checking whether spent ammo is safe to collect all re-check a few times a second instead of every frame.
- Cavalry riding slowly through its own infantry no longer knocks its own men back; only a real collision (above walking pace) does. A soldier is knocked back by a horse at most twice a second.
- Lancers galloping through the enemy no longer switch to their sword.
- Horse archers circling at the edge of their reach no longer stop and start shooting several times a second: once held back for range, they resume when the target is a few metres inside it.
- Infantry braced against a cavalry charge keeps its brace for 8 s after the charge ends, instead of breaking formation and re-forming every time the cavalry pulls back to reform.

- Starving a besieged fief works again: paid healing no longer mends starving garrisons, militia or parties faster than starvation wounds them.

- Cancelling the upgrade popup no longer forgets the spoils already promised to upgrades made before opening it.

- Men taken from a garrison or companion party and upgraded in the same visit pay with the spoils they bring. Men sent the other way take their share with them, and it is no longer spent on the upgrades of the men who stay.

- The troop upgrade cost multiplier now multiplies the price instead of stacking with perk discounts, so a low setting plus perks no longer makes upgrades free.
- Leaderless parties that belong to someone (patrols) only promote the men their own spoils cover instead of billing the owner, and AI upgrades no longer pay the supplying town more gold than the payer had.
- Prisoners recruited while in a town or village no longer arrive with a free spoils allowance.
- War Sails: AI armies that have to sail to a siege no longer board their ships, disembark and board again in an endless loop. A party's strength no longer drops while it is at sea; the landing limit for ship raids now only affects the decision to raid a village through its port.
- AI armies no longer form, march for a day and disband. A new army still needs 2.5x the defenders' strength to besiege, but one that already exists keeps its target down to 1.5x, vanilla's own factor for a siege under way. Army creation and dispersal are recorded in the garrison log.
- Clans without a culture are removed the way vanilla does it, and clans that are already eliminated are no longer destroyed again on every load.
- **Militia caps:** militia grow freely up to a soft cap of 40% of the fief's base (town prosperity, a castle's average village hearth, a village's hearth), then ever more slowly up to a hard cap of 75%. Above the hard cap 5% of the excess leaves each day, with their kit refunded. Barracks, the castle Guard House, Training Fields, the Train Militia and Raise Troops projects and the Citizenship, Cantons and Bolster the Fyrd policies raise the soft cap (up to 70%); Serfdom lowers it. The Guard House and the four policies no longer change militia per day directly. The militia tooltip shows both caps.
- A town arms new militia straight from its own market's shelves, and disbanded militiamen put their kit back on them.
- Prisoners sold at a castle are paid from the castle's own wealth, and gold the game pays to a castle goes into its wealth instead of an unused purse.
- **Workshop gear follows the town's culture:** most weapons, shields, armor and ammo a workshop makes now come from the gear of that culture's militia, troop trees and mercenaries. A market holds at most 6 of any one item, so a few items no longer flood every market, and foreign-culture gear is no longer made.
- **Leather supply:** towns no longer run out of leather, and so of armor. Workshop demand for materials is counted realistically instead of growing as inputs ran out, supply caravans now also carry leather, linen, wool, flax and iron ingots between towns, and tanning runs before every recipe that uses hides or leather, at a higher rate.
- Wool and hides are cheaper, and villages produce hides a little faster.

### Tournament
- Skipping a round simulates the right fighters again, instead of reusing the previous match and scoring nobody.
- Tournaments no longer crash on load when an overhaul mod removes vanilla's elite prize items. Unready or junk prizes are replaced.
- Tournament participants use the host town's culture, and NPC tournament tiers compare against average armor tier, as the player's do.

### Config
- All logging toggles and developer mode are now in one collapsible "RBM Debug & Logging" section. This adds rows for the AI behavior log and armor penetration messages, which had no UI before.
- A newly created config is read right away, so settings missing from the default file are saved from the first session.
- With Battle Hit Logging on, every AI bow, crossbow and sling shot is traced in the hit log (aim, predicted range, where it landed).
- Every option on the config screen now has a hover tooltip that states its default.
- "Recruits Kitted From Market" no longer shows the Starting Gold tooltip text.
- Hit stop and the developer battle stats now need RBM AI on; the battle hit log and the ranged aim arc need RBM Combat on.
- Reset to Default restores every setting to its real default. It used to set Spoils Spent on Fun to 1.5 instead of 0.25 and skipped Building Cost Multiplier, Construction Budget Share, Daily Maintenance and Self-Funded Maintenance Share.
- The "Raid Plunder Pocketed" setting is removed; it only acted as an on/off switch. Raid spoils always flow as its default did, and old configs still load.
- Options that depend on another sit directly below it, indented, and sub-groups share one heading style.
- The battle hit log records every blocked or parried melee blow by type (chamber, weapon block or parry, shield block or parry, wrong-side shield), with a per-side table at the end of the battle. The developer battle stats show a melee hits counter and a counter for each block type.
- **Changelog on the title screen:** an "RBM Changelog" badge in the top-right corner opens the list of versions and their notes. The newest one opens by itself once after an update.

- Fixed a crash at deployment in custom battles when a troop with a signature two-handed weapon was picked as banner bearer.
- Siege machines no longer crash the mission when a soldier walking to one has lost his formation, and releasing a siege engine no longer crashes on soldiers without an AI component.
- Arrows no longer crash the game when the shooter has already left the battle, whether they hit a shield worn on the back or armor (e.g. a helmet on a headshot).

- Hiring modded tavern mercenaries in your own fief no longer crashes the hire menu.
- The smithy no longer crashes on saved crafting orders whose weapon uses a part its template no longer has (e.g. the gang leader dagger quest).

## v4.5.1 (changes since v4.5.0.2)

### Combat
- RBM's managed core parameters (spear/thrust tuning) now actually load. They were never applied before. They apply only with RBM Combat enabled.
  - Swings that hit the arm deal 0.5x damage (vanilla 0.15x); thrusts that hit the arm deal 0.034x.
  - Fuller thrust and late-swing speed curves; ballista bolts have 5x drag.
- Fixed the two-handed mace skill coefficient (1.125 → 0.1125). Every swing was hitting the damage cap from around skill 25.
- Punch damage: bare fists 0.4 → 0.3, cloth/leather gloves 0.3 → 0.4. A glove no longer loses to a bare fist.
- Fixed unarmed blows while holding an item applying the skill formula twice.
- Shield impacts: blocks and parries no longer spawn body-hit dust or blood. Metal shields throw sparks on every block, and wooden shields splinter on heavy blocks.
- Restored vanilla collision reactions that RBM's armor thresholds skipped: kick and bash follow-through, hilt hits, shrugged-off blows and body punches. The couched lance keeps RBM's stick/bounce but regains the vanilla pass-through roll, so the Skewer perk works again.
- Weapon sticking and hit particles now use the blow's damage type, so a swing that doesn't land on the blade counts as blunt.

### Posture / AI
- Posture crush-through now uses the same damage math as live combat. The AI's copy dated from 2023 and had drifted from it. Crush-through damage is scaled x0.85 to compensate for the change.
- Fixed unarmed crush-through dividing by zero (NaN damage).
- Cavalry-charge knockdown routs flee in formation again. Since 4.5.0 these troops left their formation, came back formationless, and then charged on their own, ignoring orders. This was most visible in large battles. Charge routs also no longer set off the flee-contagion morale wave.
- The rally now brings back only charge-routed troops. Ordered retreats and normal morale routs are left alone.
- Fixed banner bearers added by Raise Your Banner clumping together, and bearers "attacking air". Bearers now always stand next to a neighbouring soldier in the formation grid.

### Campaign
- **New: per-settlement recruit pool.** Manpower is now a finite, regenerating resource.
  - Its size comes from prosperity (towns/castles) or hearth (villages): it grows +0.03 per point a day, up to a maximum of 0.2 per point.
  - Each new volunteer costs 1 point. Each new garrison soldier costs 1 + (garrison / soft size)², where the soft size is 150 for towns and 100 for castles.
  - Garrisons stop growing when the pool falls to a reserve of 0.07 per point, which is kept for volunteers.
  - Militia and troop transfers are unaffected.
  - Building bonuses: Train Militia / Housing daily project +25% growth, Raise Troops daily project +50%, Castellan's Office max +10/20/30%, Barracks soft size +20/40/60, a fief's Roads and Paths +10/20/30% growth for its villages.
  - The settlement tooltip and the garrison tooltip show the pool, the per-soldier garrison cost and the building bonuses.
  - Building descriptions that promised garrison capacity or automatic recruitment (which do nothing under RBM) have been rewritten.
- **AI kingdoms form armies and take fiefs again.** Their siege targets always scored 0, so armies never gathered.
  - Fresh sieges now need 2.5x strength (it was 3x).
  - AI lords top up their parties from their own clan's surplus garrisons, so they meet the party-size requirement for joining armies. Lords who already qualify aren't pulled home.
- New option: "AI Lords Buy Equipment". Turn it off if another mod manages lord gear. It is on by default. Pack trains are unaffected.
- Goods are now removed and paid for by the exact stack (including item modifiers) everywhere: citizen demand, troop food and drink, refining, minting, construction, caravans and workshops. Previously a sale could be paid for goods that never left the stack, or take from the wrong stack. A caravan that is short of cargo is now trimmed instead of creating goods from nothing.
- Recruit, upgrade and luxury market purchases pay what the stack they actually take is worth.

### Auto-resolve
- Melee profiles use the shared combat skill table, so future retunes apply to it automatically. Blunt thrusts (whips, pitchforks, training weapons) are no longer pinned at the damage cap.

### Stability / fixes
- Fixed markets getting permanently stuck with NaN prices (#22). Existing saves are repaired on the next daily tick. Town food budgets and rounding are also guarded against NaN.
- Fixed a race between the save thread and the town food cache, and a crash for towns with no owner.
- Config: decimal settings now parse and save correctly on comma-decimal locales. Previously 0.05 was read as 5 (thrust modifier, weapon-type factors, horse price).
- A config version mismatch that resets settings is now logged.

### UI
- Weapon comparison tooltip: bow, crossbow and throwable rows show the compared weapon's own values, rows stay aligned with the target column, and missing values are left blank instead of showing -100 or 0.
