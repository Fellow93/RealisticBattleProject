# RBMCampaign — campaign economy

Moved verbatim from CLAUDE.md on 2026-10-30. Keep adding design notes for this area here, not in CLAUDE.md.

**RBMCampaign** — Campaign-layer overhaul: the "spoils" troop economy, the settlement wealth ledger, the village-to-town production chain, and equipment-based strategic troop power (the auto-resolve itself is RBMAutoResolve, above). Patcher entry: `RBMCampaignPatcher.DoPatching` (calls `PatchAll` + registers the party-screen widget). References RBMConfig and RBMAutoResolve. Unlike the combat modules, most logic lives in `CampaignBehavior` subclasses, not just Harmony patches — they are added in `OnGameStart` for `Campaign` sessions (`RBM/SubModule.cs`), starting with Spoils, TroopUpkeep, `RBMCampaignSessionBehavior` (session cache resets + the hourly/before-save `BufferedLogWriter.FlushAll`; it holds the slot the auto-resolve behavior used to, and later behaviors rely on that order), Spectate, Economy, SettlementWealth. Organized into folders (namespace stays flat `RBMCampaign`; the old-style csproj lists every file with an explicit `<Compile Include>` — **update it when adding/moving files**). See `RBMCampaign/ARCHITECTURE.md` for the full file map.
- `Spoils/` — `SpoilsPool` (a `partial static class` split across several files) is a per-troop-**stack** purse in gold (1 spoils point = 1 gold), keyed by `party.Id + "#" + character.StringId`, persisted via `SyncData` key `RBM_troopSpoilsGold`. Fills from battle loot, raid/siege plunder, and the stack's **whole** daily wage; drains on upgrades, field maintenance, food, carousing, paid healing and luxuries. Surplus over the cap is **drunk in settlements** (crediting that settlement's purse), NOT returned to the owner's gold — the one spoils→gold exit is the leader's cut in `SpoilsPool.LeaderCut.cs`, which draws from the purse first and so mints nothing.
- `Upkeep/` — `TroopUpkeep` (+ `.Food`/`.Healing`/`.Luxury`) and `TroopMarketFeedback`, which lands troop spending in a settlement's purse.
- `Upgrades/` — gold-cost/tooltip patches, the reimplemented AI (`UpgradeReadyTroops`) and player-side upgrade paths, and `UpgradeSupply` (supply-town gate + market draw + payment leg).
- `Wages/TierBasedWageModel.cs` — the per-tier wage table (foot 20/30/40/60/120/240, horse 30/40/60/120/240/480). **Not configurable** — it applies whenever the module's patches are on.
- `Settlements/` — the two-pot wealth ledger (`SettlementWealth` + `SettlementGoldFunnel` over vanilla's writes), tariffs, ransoms, garrison/militia/admin/construction upkeep, workshop purses.
  - `PatrolUpkeep` (+ `.Patches.cs`, `settlementPatrolsEnabled`; also needs `SpoilsPool.IsEnabled`, since the spoils wage pass bills the patrols — off either way = vanilla patrols; log `Diagnostics/PatrolLog` → `logs/patrols/`) — every town/castle keeps one land patrol funded from its treasury (+ a town's citizen surplus via `GarrisonSubsidy.CitizenCapacity`; the owner never pays), sized `AveragePot × patrolBudgetFraction × B / perMan` (the pot averaged over 7 days — `PotAverageDays`, an exponential step in the daily pass, saved as `RBM_patrolAveragePot` — also read by the keep and raise gates; spending checks and draws the live pot) in [12, min(60, 20B)], `B = 1 + 0.5·Guard House + 0.25 edict + 0.25 Patrols perk`, template `patrol_party_<culture>_template_level_N`. It only steers vanilla's `PatrolPartiesCampaignBehavior` and the patrol/size models (keep/spawn gates, paid spawn + `Replenish` instead of the free clear-and-refill, no new-game spawn); naval patrols keep War Sails' rules but are billed daily from `SpoilsPool.Wages.cs` like militia. Manning (`Fill`, raise and refill alike): the template gives the shape, each slot is filled from the home garrison's healthy men of that troop, then of the same tier and kind (`IsMounted`/`IsRanged`), then of the same kind one tier lower (`GarrisonLowerTierSteps` 1), never below `GarrisonKeepFloor` = a fixed 50 healthy men (not a share of the size limit, which RBM lifts by 10000), moved with their XP and purse share (`SpoilsPool.TransferSpoils`); the rest are bought as the template troop at the garrison's price and out of its pool (`GarrisonRecruitCost.SpawnCostFor`, `RecruitPool.GarrisonAvailable`/`ConsumeGarrison`) but against the FUNDING POT, the money the size is worked from (pot ≥ `GarrisonSpawnReserveMult` 5 × price, ≥ keep reserve after it, and `SizeAtPot` of the pot after his price ≥ the head count he makes, so a refill never buys men the next trim sends back), paid by PatrolUpkeep's own `PayRecruitKit` via `DebitPot` (treasury then citizen surplus; a town's treasury share credited to citizens as armourers), booked `PatrolRecruit`. Never the garrison's treasury-only `CanAffordRecruit`/`PayRecruitKit`: a thin treasury with rich citizens sized big patrols and bought nobody. SPAWN/REFILL/STATE log lines carry "garrison H healthy, S spare"; STATE is written only on a real state change (has/keep/raise/short of men/level), never on a size change alone. `MissingFromTarget` matches the men a patrol has to its slots in passes (own troop → same tier+kind → same kind ≤ 2 tiers up → same kind ≤ 1 tier down → the slot troop's `UpgradeTargets` line → same kind any tier), each man one slot, so promoted men and older-template men are not re-bought. A refill (which does nothing once `CanKeepPatrol` fails) that leaves the patrol under 90% of its size stalls it (`_refillStalled`): its size limit is held at the head count it had THEN, so vanilla's AI (home until ratio ≥ 0.9) sends it out short and later losses draw it home; cleared on entering home (`SettlementEntered` postfix), when the daily pass finds it at home, or after 7 days. A refill leaving it under 12 men (`MinSize`) with nobody to be had marks it; the next daily pass stands it down (men to the garrison, `DestroyPartyAction`, vanilla's queue re-raises). The spawn-gate runs the same `Fill` as a plan (`apply: false`): the whole roster must be mannable and the pot must hold 2 × keep reserve (`SpawnKeepFloorMult`, so a garrison-manned raise is not stood down next day) + 3 × the BOUGHT men's kit (a castle 1 ×, `CastleSpawnKitReserveMult`). Men go BACK to the garrison (XP pro rata, purses via `TransferSpoils`, wounded too) when vanilla's `RemoveSettlementParties` stands a patrol down (keep-gate, or a peaceful owner change — `OnSettlementOwnerChangedEvent` prefix/finalizer marks the detail; `BySiege`/`ByRebellion` keep vanilla's destroy), and as surplus over the current size when it rests at home (`TrimSurplus`: unmatched men, then lowest tier; daily + on entering home); never while the patrol or fief is in a MapEvent/siege. Land patrols get +0.5 base map speed (`SpeedBonus`, a `DefaultPartySpeedCalculatingModel.CalculateBaseSpeed` postfix) and, without a town Guard House (castles always), vanilla's level-0 patrol security +0.5/day (`DefaultSettlementSecurityModel.CalculateSecurityChange` postfix). ⚠️ The speed model reads `PartySizeLimit` inside the parallel movement tick: the plain size query only reads `_partyLimit` (ConcurrentDictionary, written on the main thread by `RefreshLimit` at session launch / daily / spawn / refill / stall / trim; an off-thread miss returns max(6, head count) and writes nothing); only the tooltip (`includeDescriptions`) computes. The main module's always-on `RBM/PatrolReplenishGuard.cs` (`com.rbmmain`, Priority.Last, reads `__runOriginal`) skips vanilla's `ReplenishParty` when the template is null, so a castle / no-Guard-House patrol left on the map with the feature off cannot NRE (nothing can guard a save once RBM is removed). Only the averaged pot is saved (`RBM_patrolAveragePot`, `PatrolUpkeep.SyncData` from the settlement-wealth behaviour); the rest is session state.
- `Production/` — village production, villager convoys/deliveries, town food supply and storage, citizen and workshop demand.
- `Economy/` — market prices and liquidity, caravan capital, recruit supply, trade-good values.
- `Power/` — `StrategicTroopPower` (equipment-based party strength, priced through RBMAutoResolve's kit model; its `Enabled` also needs `rbmCombatEnabled` — with RBM Combat off the game's own strength is used, there is no combat-off pricing path, and `ArmyStrengthFloorPatch`'s 700 falls back to vanilla's 1000) and the strategic-AI gates (`SiegeDecisionGate` etc.). `Spectate/` — no-agent AI-vs-AI spectator battle.
- `MercenaryCompanies/` — neutral hire-only mercenary companies (`mercenaryCompaniesEnabled`): two per main culture, each its own runtime minor clan (StringId prefix `rbm_merc_co_`, created on the first campaign hour so old saves get them) with one leader and one party. They never join a kingdom on their own (vanilla barters, decision proposals, minor-faction spawning and the hire dialog are blocked for them); an army leader hires one for gold (AI on `ArmyCreated`, the player from the army management screen, with injected price/cart-gold widgets), and an hourly `MercenaryContracts.Reconcile` takes the company back out of the kingdom once its party no longer serves in that kingdom's army (never mid-battle/siege, nor while its party is lost and its leader is the employer's enemy's captive). Excluded from `MercenaryContractPay`. ⚠️ Every patch must stay a no-op for clans that are not companies (`MercenaryCompanies.IsCompany`).
- `UI/` — `RBMTroopSpoilsBarWidget` (a `FillBarVerticalWidget`) and the inventory weight column, both injected into native prefabs. `SpoilsBarPrefabPatch` also injects `RBMTroopHoverTooltipWidget` (party-screen troop-row hover → troop tooltip, gated on `rbmCampaignEnabled` + `troopPerksEnabled`) into `PartyTroopTuple.xml` and `PartyTroopTupleLeft.xml`; the PartyScreen codegen skip runs when either the spoils bar or the hover is on.
- `Diagnostics/` — `SpoilsLog` (`logs/campaign/`), `EconomyLog` (`logs/economy/`), `MercenaryLog` (`logs/mercenaries/`, the mercenary companies' lifecycle; log them here, never through SpoilsLog), `StuckPartyLog` (`logs/stuckparties/`, fed by the observer `StuckPartyLogBehavior`: hourly pass over `MobileParty.All`, one STUCK line per party with a move target that stays within 1 map unit for 24 h, incl. the engine's private `_aiPathNotFound`); each gated by its own config toggle (the auto-resolve's `SimulationLog` is in RBMAutoResolve). `TestBattleCommand` is the debug console command `campaign.rbm_test_battle [countPerTroop=5] [attacker=<groups>] [defender=<groups>] [player=attacker|defender]` (all-troop-types field battle, per-side culture/mercenary/minor-faction filters; the player's party is snapshotted and restored when the encounter is over, polled from `RBM.SubModule.OnApplicationTick`). With no parameters it opens a vanilla-inquiry setup (`TestBattleCommand.Setup.cs`: Default → count, Custom → attacker groups → defender groups → side → count → start or save as preset, Last used / Load preset → count, Delete presets; presets in `TestBattleCommand.Presets.cs` are one XML per preset in `Configs/RBM/TestBattlePresets/` plus `last_used_test_battle.xml`, written on every start, like the custom battle presets); each step opens on the next tick, never inside the previous popup's callback (the popup closes itself after the callback). Every campaign log writes through `BufferedLogWriter` (one open buffered handle per file, flushed hourly/before save by `RBMCampaignSessionBehavior`, closed on game end by `RBM.SubModule`) and prunes with `LogRetention` — both in `RBMConfig/Shared/Logging/` (namespace `RBMConfig.Logging`), shared with RBMAutoResolve, whose `SimulationLog` flushes its own writer so it works with RBM Campaign off. Never add a per-line `File.AppendAllText` or a `Debug.Print` copy of log lines.
